Data: National Inventory of Dams (NID), U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, national GeoPackage, data updated 20 November 2026; US government data, public domain. The NID lists dams that meet its size or hazard thresholds (generally 25 ft high and 15 acre-feet of storage, or 6 ft and 50 acre-feet, or a high or significant hazard rating), not every pond. Shown: 73,573 of 92,512 dams; 18,902 have no recorded completion year and are not on the timeline; 37 in US territories (outside the 50 states and DC) are left out. Dates are the year completed; many dams were raised or rebuilt later. The spikes at round years (1950 has about three times as many dams as 1949 or 1951, and 1960, 1965 and 1970 stand out too) most likely come from estimated completion years recorded as round numbers. Main purpose is the NID's primary purpose, grouped. Storage is the NID storage (the maximum the reservoir can hold), in cubic kilometres (1 acre-foot = 1,233.48 m³), counted once per reservoir: dikes and levees listed with a main dam, and repeated entries for the same reservoir (such as the seven sections of the Herbert Hoover Dike), are not added again, and the Soo Locks are left out (their storage is Lake Superior, a natural lake). Natural lakes raised or held back by dams, such as Lake Okeechobee and Devils Lake, are counted as the NID lists them.
