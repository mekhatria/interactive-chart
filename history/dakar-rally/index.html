Every Dakar Rally Route, 1979–2026

From Paris to Dakar across the Sahara, to South America after the 2008 edition was cancelled, and since 2020 through Saudi Arabia: each edition's route lights up stop by stop, then stays on the map as a trace.

2008: cancelledThe rally was called off on 4 January 2008, the day before the start in Lisbon, over safety concerns in Mauritania after four French tourists were killed there. From 2009 it moved to South America.

Routes: the stage tables (start and finish town of each day) of every edition's article on English Wikipedia, or on French Wikipedia for 1983–1989 and 1994 (Rallye Dakar), CC BY-SA 4.0; this route data is shared under the same licence. Town positions: the coordinates of the linked Wikipedia articles, a few from Wikidata. The lines are smooth curves through the overnight stops in order; they are not the tracks driven. A few overnight stops could not be placed from a source and are skipped, so the line runs straight past them: 1981 Moudjbara; 1982 Dara, Tiougoune; 1983 Chebaba, Tiougoune; 1988 Lemjebir; 1993 Bir Amrane; 1997 Oclan; 2000 Waha; 2001 El Ghallaouiya; 2003 Abu Rish, Sarir, Zilla; 2024 Al Salamiya; 2026 Bivouac Refuge. Countries use today's borders (Highcharts world map). Ferry crossings and the 2000 airlift from Niamey to Libya are dashed and not counted in the distance. · Highcharts.com
